# Exterior cosmic motion

Planet anchors, destination IDs, approach/contact transforms and terrain orientation are preserved. Moving moons are decorative children, absent from destination selection and collision contracts. The existing non-approach body spin remains 0.003 rad/s; anchored terrain does not acquire spin. Clouds rotate their noise coordinates at 0.012 rad/s at the equator, with latitude shear of ±0.004 rad/s. Giant bands advect at 0.018 ±0.006 rad/s. These are deliberately accelerated presentation rates, not astronomical ephemerides. Moon radii are 3.05, 3.77 and 4.49 body radii, inclined 0.22–0.48 radians, with angular rates proportional to radius^-3/2 (innermost period about 74 seconds). Their own spin is 0.025 rad/s.

All motion uses the existing scene clock or shared fixed-step accretion state. Repeated updates at the same time and camera pose freeze the visual state. Camera movement still changes the view of a paused scene. No new loop, global state, texture download, render target or screen readback is introduced. Moons use shared geometry/material, with 1/2/3 visible on Battery/Balanced/High–Ultra and distance culling. The supplemental stellar catalogue has 24/48/96 sources and at most twice as many images, with fixed typed-array storage. Its resources remain attached to the anomaly and are covered by the engine's existing final scene traversal disposal. Shared moon resources remain attached to fixed planets even when sectors are evicted.

The ordinary-flight black hole now has differentially moving disk filaments, a warmer outer disk and bright inner emission, broad upper and compressed lower repeated disk images, and a narrow critical-curve illustration. Direct and repeated images evaluate the same emission function and the same eight accretion-source uniforms used by the optional exact Schwarzschild observation. The disk phase is theta − 1.4 t (3/r)^1.5; time is accelerated presentation time, with no retarded emission delay. Source radius maps 3–9 to exterior radius 1.2–4.8 as before.

Baseline screenshot review showed a washed-out near-face-on radial target. A targeted composition correction flips the fixed disk X tilt from −1.1 to +1.1 radians, giving the normal origin/Orpheus-to-Wound direction approximately 9.5 degrees elevation above its plane while preserving the existing arrival route. Gas, experiment geometry and exact-observation source orientation use this same quaternion. Peak illustrative emission and concentric-band contrast are reduced, and outer emission falls faster, leaving the foreground disk separate from the upper repeated image. Other approach directions can still correctly see a face-on disk; the plane never tracks the camera. Final renderer screenshots remain the acceptance authority.

Emission asymmetry uses the disk tangent dotted with the observer direction, rather than a fixed screen-side gradient. The speed proxy beta = [2(r−1)]^-1/2 is the circular-emitter speed measured by a static Schwarzschild observer. The illustrated gain is [sqrt(1−1/r) sqrt(1−beta²)/(1−beta dot(tangent, view))]^4, clipped for display. Its outgoing direction is approximated as straight. The repeated-image warp is an inclination-dependent image-space compression, **not geodesic tracing**, and therefore its frequency transfer is qualitative. The unit dark sphere represents the apparent shadow; it is not a measured horizon radius. No Kerr spin or GRMHD claim is made.

Supplemental fixed distant stars use the thin point-lens equation theta − 1/theta = beta in Einstein-radius units, producing opposite-parity image pairs with the inner image occulted by the shadow when appropriate. This is a weak-field illustration with an art-chosen Einstein radius of 1.65 shadow units, not the exact strong-field stellar sky. It does not distort or sample the existing main starfield. Smooth, extended Einstein arcs and flux-calibrated magnification are not modeled. The exact observation in `relativity.ts` and CPU `relativity-model.ts` is unchanged.

Physical visual motivation: [NASA/JPL-Caltech's qualitative Schwarzschild visualization](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14619/) describes differential rotation, Doppler brightening, shadow versus horizon, and repeated disk images. [Bruneton's beam-tracing/LUT renderer](https://ebruneton.github.io/black_hole_shader/) is a higher-fidelity reference, not the algorithm implemented here.

Orpheus's kind-8 globe is recessed below the water owner's 51–77-unit seabed within the 30,000-unit horizontal world-north cap. The existing 0.9996 scale and 70-unit additional radial recess give 89.172 units total. The cap smoothly restores the original globe outside that region (north cosine 0.780625 down to 0.70). World north is inverse-transformed through the preserved X=0.5 body rotation; no water or contact frame is rotated.
